# Five-minute demo — Cambridge Storefront Opportunity

## 0:00–0:30 — Problem and user

“The Cambridge Community Development Department has limited time and small-business support resources. This site answers a focused question: which commercial districts and space types should staff investigate first? It is a prioritization tool, not a promise that a particular tenant will succeed.”

Show the homepage title and the September 2025 decision snapshot.

## 0:30–1:30 — Data and method

“The analysis combines Cambridge’s public vacant-storefront snapshots and cottage-food permits with Boston food-license context and county-level Census and BLS industry data. Every displayed number is generated by a Python pipeline. Phone numbers, names, emails, home addresses, and home coordinates were removed before any deliverable was written.”

Open **Method**. Point out the July midpoint assumption for year-only vacancy dates and the definition of the priority score: storefront count × estimated average vacancy months.

## 1:30–3:30 — Three live interactions

1. Click **Explore**, then uncheck **East Cambridge** under Commercial districts. Show that the storefront count and chart update and that the URL changes.
2. Click the **Districts** view control. Compare the filtered count bars and average-duration color; explain why count and duration should be considered together.
3. Set **Minimum estimated vacancy** to 36 months, then click **Vacancy profile**. Point out which size bands remain in the persistent-vacancy group.

Optional if time allows: click **Map**, choose **FT** under License category, and explain that the map is Boston context only—not a Cambridge competitor census.

## 3:30–4:30 — Findings and recommendations

“The latest file contains 96 reported storefronts across 10 normalized districts. Central Square ranks first on the combined count-duration score. Of the records with a vacancy year, 79—82.3 percent—are estimated to exceed one year. The data supports three actions: focus the first outreach cycle on Central and Harvard Squares; pilot small-space assistance for the 13 observed long-term spaces under 1,500 square feet; and create an opt-in path from cottage-food permitting to shared kitchens or storefront testing.”

Return to **Decision brief** and show the recommendations and ranked district table.

## 4:30–5:00 — Limits

Click **Limitations**.

“These data can prioritize investigation, but they cannot explain why a unit is vacant, measure rent or customer demand, or prove that support will cause occupancy. The next responsible evidence would add rents, foot traffic, accessibility, and opt-in stakeholder feedback.”
